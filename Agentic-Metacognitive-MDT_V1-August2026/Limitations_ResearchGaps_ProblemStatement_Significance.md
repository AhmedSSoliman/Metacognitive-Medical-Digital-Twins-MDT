# Limitations of Previous Work, Research Gaps, Problem Statement, and Significance

---

## 1. Limitations of Previous Work

Six structural limitations define the boundaries of the current state of the art:

### 1.1 Post-Hoc Safety Without Structural Guarantees
- Every existing system evaluates safety **after** generation rather than constraining what can be generated.
- DT-ICU: safety is emergent from supervised training on observed outcomes; no mechanism verifies that a novel prediction is physiologically admissible before surfacing.
  - *Medical example:* DT-ICU trained on a population where **dobutamine** (a drug that increases the heart's pumping strength) was rarely co-administered with high-dose **vasopressin** (a drug that constricts blood vessels to raise blood pressure) could generate a trajectory combining both at maximum doses in a patient with severe **aortic stenosis** (a narrowing of the heart's main outflow valve). This combination risks **acute left ventricular outflow obstruction** (physically blocking blood from leaving the heart), because the model never learned to flag it and no pre-generation constraint exists to catch it.
- DT-GPT: authors acknowledge explanatory outputs can hallucinate and are not guaranteed to reflect the true basis for a prediction.
  - *Medical example:* DT-GPT could forecast improving **hemodynamics** (the circulation of blood through the cardiovascular system) for a post-cardiac surgery patient while citing "resolving inflammation" in its explanatory output, when the actual driver of the forecast is a statistical pattern unrelated to the stated reasoning, and the patient is in fact developing **cardiac tamponade** (compression of the heart caused by fluid accumulating in the sac surrounding it, preventing it from filling properly) masked by vasopressor support.
- MIRA: safety rests on physician audit of sampled cases after the fact against predefined checklist domains.
  - *Medical example:* MIRA could recommend discharging an emergency department patient with chest pain after a negative initial **troponin** (a protein released into the blood when heart muscle is damaged; the primary blood test for diagnosing heart attacks). This misses a **Type 2 NSTEMI** (a heart attack caused by oxygen supply-demand mismatch rather than a blocked artery, where troponin may rise on a delayed timeline). The physician audit catches this only if that particular case falls within the sampled audit set; if it does not, the recommendation reaches the clinical record unchecked.
- DeepCARS: safety depends on clinician response to alerts, not on any property of the score itself.
  - *Medical example:* DeepCARS triggers a **cardiac arrest** (sudden cessation of heart function) risk alert for a patient on a **step-down unit** (an intermediate care area between the ICU and a general ward) at 3:00 AM, but the overnight nurse, managing six patients simultaneously, does not review the alert for 40 minutes. The system generated a correct signal, but the safety outcome depends entirely on human response latency rather than on any property of the alert itself.
- Data poisoning studies demonstrate corrupted models pass standard benchmarks undetected.
- Backdoor evaluations confirm adversarially trained models behave normally on all validation inputs except specific triggers.
- **Implication:** audit-only safety is structurally insufficient as a guarantee.

### 1.2 Accuracy-Reasoning Dissociation
- Frontier models achieving 60-70% diagnostic accuracy exhibit no schema-scale consistency in reasoning traces across similar cases.
  - *Medical example:* a model correctly diagnoses **pulmonary embolism** (a blood clot lodged in the lung's arteries, blocking blood flow) in two clinically similar patients (both presenting with **tachycardia** [abnormally fast heart rate, typically >100 beats per minute], **dyspnea** [difficulty breathing], and elevated **D-dimer** [a blood test measuring clot breakdown products; elevated levels suggest active clotting]), but the reasoning trace for Patient A invokes **Wells criteria** (a standardized clinical scoring system for estimating PE probability) and **CT angiography** (a contrast-enhanced CT scan that directly visualizes blood clots in lung arteries) findings, while the trace for Patient B invokes pneumonia differential and incidental CT findings, with no structural similarity between the two correct reasoning paths.
- Reasoning-graph similarity does not differ between correct and incorrect diagnoses; accuracy and reasoning structure are empirically separable.
  - *Medical example:* a model incorrectly diagnoses a patient with **diabetic ketoacidosis** (DKA; a life-threatening complication of diabetes in which the body produces excess ketone acids, causing severe metabolic acidosis) as having **sepsis** (a dysregulated immune response to infection causing organ dysfunction), but the reasoning trace for this incorrect diagnosis is structurally indistinguishable from the trace of a correct sepsis diagnosis in a different patient, following the same pattern of citing tachycardia, **leukocytosis** (elevated white blood cell count, a marker of immune activation), and **metabolic acidosis** (dangerously low blood pH caused by acid accumulation). The reasoning "looks right" regardless of whether the conclusion is right.
- Chain-of-thought explanations have been shown to be unfaithful to the computation that produced the answer.
  - *Medical example:* a model recommends initiating **heparin** (a blood-thinning medication used to prevent and treat blood clots) **anticoagulation** for a patient with suspected **deep vein thrombosis** (DVT; a blood clot forming in a deep vein, typically in the leg) and generates a chain-of-thought citing **Wells score** components, but the actual computation driving the recommendation is a statistical correlation between the word "swelling" and heparin orders in the training data, not a genuine evaluation of **thrombotic risk** (risk of forming new clots) versus **bleeding risk** in this specific patient.
- Frontier models can perform invisible reasoning through semantically irrelevant filler tokens.
- **Implication:** no existing system addresses this dissociation; systems either expose no reasoning trace or expose one with no verification mechanism.

### 1.3 No Longitudinal Context Management
- No reviewed system is evaluated against a continuously evolving clinical course over days to weeks.
  - *Medical example:* a patient admitted with **community-acquired pneumonia** (a lung infection contracted outside the hospital) develops **hospital-acquired acute kidney injury** (AKI; a sudden decline in kidney function occurring during hospitalization, measured by rising **creatinine**, a waste product normally filtered by the kidneys) on day 4, followed by gradual **hepatic dysfunction** (declining liver function, measured by rising **bilirubin**, **ALT**, and **AST**, enzymes released when liver cells are damaged) by day 7. No existing system is evaluated on its ability to recognize this trajectory as **multi-organ dysfunction syndrome** (MODS; the progressive failure of two or more organ systems, carrying mortality rates of 40-100% depending on the number of organs involved) rather than treating each organ system's decline as an independent event.
- The lost-in-the-middle phenomenon demonstrates pronounced positional decay in long-context tasks.
  - *Medical example:* a **vancomycin trough level** (the lowest concentration of the antibiotic vancomycin in the blood, measured just before the next dose to ensure the drug is within a therapeutic range, typically 15-20 mcg/mL) drawn on day 5 of a 12-day admission sits in the "middle" of the clinical context. By day 12, the model has effectively lost this data point, and recommends continuing vancomycin at the original dose despite the day-5 trough of 28 mcg/mL (**supratherapeutic**, meaning above the safe range, risking **nephrotoxicity**, or kidney damage caused by the drug).
- A hindsight-annotated ICU benchmark confirmed memory-augmented agents exhibit **anchoring bias** (the tendency to rely disproportionately on the first piece of information encountered) toward early patient impressions.
  - *Medical example:* a patient admitted with a primary diagnosis of **COPD exacerbation** (a sudden worsening of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, causing increased breathlessness and cough) develops **new-onset atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response** (an irregular heart rhythm originating in the upper chambers of the heart, causing the ventricles to beat dangerously fast, often >150 beats per minute) on day 3. A model anchored to the initial admission diagnosis continues to frame tachycardia as "likely secondary to respiratory distress" rather than recognizing a new, independently actionable **arrhythmia** (abnormal heart rhythm) requiring **rate control** (medications such as diltiazem or amiodarone to slow the heart rate).
- Most agentic systems are evaluated on single-encounter snapshots.
- **Implication:** behavior over actual ICU timescales (7-14 days) remains uncharacterized.

### 1.4 Pairwise Physiological Representations
- Existing systems use pairwise knowledge graphs that cannot encode joint, multi-variable interactions.
- **Septic shock** (a severe, life-threatening form of sepsis in which infection-driven inflammation causes dangerously low blood pressure and organ failure despite fluid resuscitation) involves simultaneous **tachycardia**, **hypotension** (abnormally low blood pressure, typically systolic <90 mmHg or MAP <65 mmHg), and **hyperlactatemia** (elevated blood lactate >2 mmol/L, indicating that tissues are not receiving adequate oxygen and have shifted to anaerobic metabolism); a pairwise graph cannot represent that the three-way co-occurrence is physiologically distinct from any two-variable subset.
  - *Medical example:* a pairwise graph encodes "hypotension is associated with tachycardia" and "hypotension is associated with elevated lactate" as two separate edges. A patient presenting with hypotension and tachycardia alone (possibly **hypovolemia** [insufficient blood volume] from GI bleeding, treated with **volume resuscitation** [intravenous fluid administration to restore circulating volume]) is represented identically to a patient with hypotension, tachycardia, and hyperlactatemia (possibly **distributive shock** [a type of shock caused by abnormal distribution of blood flow due to widespread vasodilation, most commonly from sepsis], requiring **vasopressors** [medications such as norepinephrine that constrict blood vessels to raise blood pressure] and broad-spectrum antibiotics). The treatment for one is dangerous for the other: aggressive fluid resuscitation in a patient with **sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy** (weakening of the heart muscle caused by the inflammatory response to sepsis) can precipitate **acute pulmonary edema** (fluid flooding the lungs, causing respiratory failure).
- No existing safety constraint operates at the level of multi-variable state combinations.
  - *Medical example:* the combination of rising **INR** (International Normalized Ratio; a measure of how long blood takes to clot, with higher values indicating impaired clotting), falling **platelets** (cell fragments responsible for blood clotting; normal range 150,000-400,000/mcL), and elevated **fibrin degradation products** (FDPs; fragments released when the body breaks down blood clots) constitutes **disseminated intravascular coagulation** (DIC; a catastrophic condition in which the blood clotting system activates uncontrollably throughout the body, simultaneously forming tiny clots that block small blood vessels and consuming clotting factors, leading to severe bleeding). Each variable individually might be flagged by a single-variable threshold, but a system that cannot represent their joint occurrence cannot distinguish DIC from isolated **thrombocytopenia** (low platelet count, which can be a benign drug side effect) co-occurring with isolated INR elevation (**warfarin** therapy, an intentional anticoagulant effect), two situations requiring opposite interventions (DIC requires treating the underlying cause and potentially administering clotting factors; drug-induced thrombocytopenia requires stopping the offending drug; warfarin-related INR elevation may require no intervention at all).
- **Implication:** no existing safety constraint operates at the level of multi-variable physiological state combinations.

### 1.5 Communication-Agnostic Output
- No reviewed system adapts output to health literacy, emotional state, or clinical role.
  - *Medical example:* a system generating a summary for the family of a patient with severe **traumatic brain injury** (TBI; physical damage to the brain caused by an external force) produces: "**GCS** 6T [Glasgow Coma Scale, a 3-15 point scoring system for consciousness level; 6T means severely impaired consciousness in an intubated patient], **ICP** 28 mmHg [intracranial pressure, the pressure inside the skull; normal is <20 mmHg; 28 indicates dangerous swelling] with **plateau waves** [sudden, sustained spikes in intracranial pressure lasting 5-20 minutes, indicating severely impaired ability of the brain to regulate its own pressure], **midline shift** 8mm on repeat CT [displacement of brain structures from their normal central position, caused by swelling or bleeding pushing the brain to one side; >5mm is clinically significant], requiring **EVD** placement [external ventricular drain, a catheter inserted through the skull into the brain's ventricles to drain cerebrospinal fluid and reduce pressure] and consideration of **decompressive craniectomy** [surgical removal of a portion of the skull to allow the swelling brain to expand outward rather than compressing inward]." A neurosurgeon reads this in seconds. The patient's spouse, who has never heard the term "midline shift," receives the same output and understands nothing except that it sounds catastrophic.
- Uniform across the landscape: DT-ICU (risk scores), DT-GPT (fixed-register forecasts), MIRA (single-format notes), BEACON (untailored alerts).
  - *Medical example:* a BEACON-style alert fires for a patient meeting early sepsis criteria. The alert reaches both the attending **intensivist** (a physician specializing in critical care medicine) (who needs a terse "**Sepsis-3** criteria met: **qSOFA** 2 [Quick Sequential Organ Failure Assessment, a bedside screening tool scoring mental status, respiratory rate, and blood pressure; score of 2 or more suggests organ dysfunction], lactate 3.1, blood cultures pending") and a covering **intern** (a first-year resident physician) on their first ICU night (who needs the same information plus guidance on the **1-hour bundle** [a time-sensitive protocol requiring blood cultures, lactate measurement, broad-spectrum antibiotics, IV fluid resuscitation with 30 mL/kg crystalloid, and vasopressors if **MAP** (mean arterial pressure, the average blood pressure during one cardiac cycle; target >=65 mmHg in sepsis) remains below 65 after fluids], all to be initiated within 60 minutes of sepsis recognition). Both receive an identical, fixed alert.
- Clinical communication research establishes that tailoring to health literacy eliminates disparities in comprehension.
- **Implication:** the clinical AI safety literature and health-literacy literature remain entirely disconnected.

### 1.6 Evaluation Fragmentation
- Systems are evaluated on different datasets, cohorts, outcomes, and metrics; direct comparison is impossible.
  - *Medical example:* DT-ICU reports **AUROC** (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve; a metric ranging from 0.5 [random chance] to 1.0 [perfect discrimination] measuring how well a model distinguishes between patients who will and will not experience an outcome) for 24-hour **mortality prediction** on MIMIC-IV medical ICU patients. MIRA reports **diagnostic accuracy** (the proportion of cases in which the model's diagnosis matches the reference standard diagnosis) on 574 emergency department encounters. DeepCARS reports **sensitivity** (the proportion of actual positive cases that the model correctly identifies; also called the true positive rate) for in-hospital cardiac arrest at 24-hour lead time on a prospective Korean cohort. A hospital system trying to select between these three systems for its surgical ICU has no basis for comparison, because none were evaluated on the same patient population, outcome definition, or time horizon.
- No standard evaluation framework detects the failure modes above.
- A system could pass every current benchmark while exhibiting context decay, generating physiologically impossible transitions, and communicating identically to all recipients.
  - *Medical example:* a model achieves 0.91 AUROC on MIMIC-IV mortality prediction and passes **USMLE Step 3** (the third and final examination in the United States Medical Licensing Examination series, testing whether a physician can apply medical knowledge to patient management) at the 80th percentile. On deployment, it recommends **potassium supplementation** for a **hyperkalemic** (abnormally high blood potassium, >5.0 mEq/L; severe hyperkalemia [>6.0 mEq/L] can cause fatal cardiac arrhythmias) patient (K+ 6.2 mEq/L) whose earlier **hypokalemia** (abnormally low blood potassium, <3.5 mEq/L; can cause muscle weakness and dangerous arrhythmias) on admission day 1 (K+ 3.1 mEq/L) is still the most recent potassium value in the model's effective context window due to positional decay. No existing benchmark tests for this failure mode.
- **Implication:** current benchmarks are structurally inadequate to the failure modes that matter for safe deployment.

## 2. Research Gaps

Four gaps emerge from the limitations above, none addressed by any existing system:

1. **No pre-generation physiological constraint.**
   - No system embeds a formally defined, data-derived constraint into the generation process.
   - No output can be rejected as physiologically inadmissible before reaching a clinician.
   - Available safety mechanisms are either emergent (learned during training) or external (post-hoc audit); neither guarantees validity of novel outputs.
   - *Medical example:* a model generates a recommendation to administer IV **calcium gluconate** (a calcium supplement given intravenously to stabilize the heart membrane against the effects of hyperkalemia) concurrently with IV **sodium bicarbonate** (an alkalinizing agent that shifts potassium from the blood into cells) through the same IV line in a patient with **hyperkalemia**. Both drugs are individually correct for hyperkalemia, but **co-infusion causes calcium carbonate precipitation** (the two drugs react chemically in the IV tubing, forming an insoluble calcium carbonate precipite that can block the line and, if infused, cause vascular occlusion). No existing system can reject this recommendation at generation time because no system encodes **drug-interaction constraints** as a property of the generation process.

2. **No verification of reasoning-trace fidelity.**
   - No mechanism distinguishes genuine reasoning revision from superficial self-correction (restating conclusions in hedged language without underlying representational change).
   - No existing reward signal, evaluation metric, or architectural mechanism detects this failure mode.
   - *Medical example:* a model initially recommends aggressive **diuresis** (administration of diuretics such as furosemide to remove excess fluid from the body through increased urine production) for a patient with **pulmonary edema** (fluid accumulation in the lungs, causing impaired gas exchange and respiratory distress), then generates a "self-correction" stating "Upon reflection, the patient's low **ejection fraction** (EF; the percentage of blood the left ventricle pumps out with each contraction; normal is 55-70%; low EF [<40%] indicates the heart is too weak to pump effectively) suggests caution with aggressive diuresis." However, the model's revised recommendation is identical (same **furosemide** dose, same target urine output), and the "reflection" is a textual performance of caution without any change in the underlying clinical plan. A clinician reading the trace might trust the system more because it appeared to self-correct, when in fact nothing changed.

3. **No context management evaluated at ICU timescales.**
   - No system incorporates a mechanism specifically designed to retain physiologically salient history as data accumulates beyond an LLM's effective context window.
   - No evaluation measures context retention as a distinct endpoint over multi-day admissions.
   - *Medical example:* a patient with **cirrhosis** (chronic, irreversible scarring of the liver, impairing its ability to filter toxins, produce proteins, and regulate metabolism) admitted on day 1 with a **MELD score** of 18 (Model for End-Stage Liver Disease; a scoring system ranging from 6-40 that predicts 90-day mortality in liver disease based on bilirubin, creatinine, and INR; a score of 18 indicates moderate disease with approximately 6% 90-day mortality) is started on **lactulose** (an osmotic laxative used in liver disease to reduce blood ammonia levels by trapping ammonia in the gut for excretion) and **rifaximin** (a non-absorbable antibiotic that reduces ammonia-producing bacteria in the gut) for **hepatic encephalopathy** (a syndrome of confusion, personality changes, and eventually coma caused by the accumulation of toxins, particularly **ammonia**, that a failing liver can no longer clear from the blood). By day 9, the model has lost the baseline MELD score, the initial lactulose dosing, and the day-3 **ammonia level** (a blood test measuring the concentration of ammonia; normal <35 mcmol/L; elevation indicates the liver is failing to clear this neurotoxin). When the patient develops new confusion on day 9, the model attributes it to "altered mental status, likely metabolic" rather than recognizing it as recurrent hepatic encephalopathy in a patient with known cirrhosis whose lactulose was inadvertently held for 48 hours during a procedure.

4. **No integration of safety and communication adaptation.**
   - No system infers the cognitive or emotional state of its recipient or adapts communication accordingly.
   - Whether joint optimization of physiological safety and communication adaptation produces trade-offs or synergies has never been studied.
   - *Medical example:* a system generates a physiologically correct and safe recommendation to transition a patient from **curative-intent treatment** (medical interventions aimed at curing or controlling the disease) to **comfort measures only** (CMO; a care approach focused exclusively on relieving pain and suffering, discontinuing interventions such as mechanical ventilation, vasopressors, and dialysis that prolong the dying process without improving quality of life). The recommendation is delivered in the same clinical register to: (a) the **palliative care attending** (a physician specializing in symptom management and end-of-life care), who needs a terse summary of the clinical rationale; (b) the patient's adult daughter, who is hearing for the first time that her parent is dying and needs the information delivered with language calibrated to her emotional state and health literacy. The physiological safety of the recommendation is identical in both cases, but the communication failure in case (b) could lead the family to reject the recommendation, request **futile interventions** (medical treatments that cannot achieve the desired physiological goal and only prolong the dying process), and prolong suffering, an outcome that is clinically unsafe despite the recommendation itself being physiologically sound.

## 3. Problem Statement

The integration of agentic AI into ICU workflows is constrained by a **Socio-Technical Gap**: a mismatch between what current systems can generate and what is required for that output to be trusted and safely acted upon. This gap manifests as four convergent inadequacies:

1. **Longitudinal context decay leading to mechanistic hallucination.**
   - Models progressively lose track of a patient's evolving baseline over multi-day admissions.
   - Degraded context combined with statistically plausible text generation produces recommendations that are linguistically fluent yet physiologically dangerous.
   - *Medical example:* a 68-year-old patient admitted with **acute decompensated heart failure** (ADHF; a sudden worsening of heart failure symptoms, typically presenting with severe breathlessness and fluid overload, with **EF 20%**, meaning the heart pumps only 20% of its blood volume per beat versus the normal 55-70%) receives aggressive IV **diuresis** and improves over days 1-3. On day 7, the patient develops new hypotension. The model, having lost context of the underlying severe **cardiomyopathy** (disease of the heart muscle that impairs its ability to pump) and the preceding diuresis, recommends a 2-liter **normal saline bolus** (rapid intravenous infusion of 0.9% sodium chloride solution to increase circulating blood volume) for "volume depletion," a recommendation that is statistically common in clinical text for hypotension but could precipitate **flash pulmonary edema** (sudden, severe flooding of the lungs with fluid, causing acute respiratory failure) and **respiratory arrest** in this specific patient. The correct intervention (initiating low-dose **vasopressor** support while holding diuretics) requires knowledge of the full 7-day trajectory.

2. **Opaque reasoning without verification.**
   - Systems function as passive assistants without inspectable, verified reasoning traces.
   - A sepsis alert exposing no chain of evidence forces the bedside clinician to re-derive the reasoning from the chart.
   - The verification burden shifts back onto the clinician rather than being reduced.
   - *Medical example:* a model generates a high-probability alert for **ventilator-associated pneumonia** (VAP; a lung infection that develops 48+ hours after a patient is placed on **mechanical ventilation**, a machine that breathes for the patient through a tube inserted into the airway). The alert does not specify whether it was triggered by new **infiltrate on chest X-ray** (an abnormal white shadow on the lung image suggesting fluid, pus, or inflammation), rising **white blood cell count** (an indicator of immune response to infection), change in **sputum character** (the mucus coughed up or suctioned from the airway; purulent [thick, yellow-green] sputum suggests infection), or **fever** (body temperature >38.0 degrees C). The bedside nurse must independently verify each criterion, review 72 hours of **microbiology results** (culture and sensitivity reports identifying which bacteria are present and which antibiotics can kill them), and determine whether the alert reflects a genuine new infection or **colonization** (the presence of bacteria without active infection, which does not require treatment). The time spent re-deriving the reasoning (15-20 minutes) is time not spent at the bedside of the six other patients in the nurse's assignment.

3. **No pre-generation safety constraint.**
   - No system constrains the physiological admissibility of outputs at generation time.
   - The strongest comparator relies on physician audit of sampled outputs; the weakest provide no safety evaluation beyond aggregate benchmark performance.
   - In a domain where a single impossible recommendation can cause irreversible harm, this is a fundamental architectural gap.
   - *Medical example:* a model recommends initiating a **beta-blocker** (**metoprolol** 25 mg IV; a class of drugs that slow the heart rate and reduce blood pressure by blocking the effects of adrenaline on the heart) for **rate control** in a patient presenting with tachycardia, hypotension, and **wheezing** (a high-pitched whistling sound during breathing, caused by narrowed airways). The recommendation is linguistically standard for tachycardia management. However, this patient has **acute severe asthma** (a life-threatening asthma attack causing severe airway constriction and respiratory failure) with **compensatory tachycardia** (the heart beating fast to maintain oxygen delivery despite impaired breathing) and early **obstructive shock** (cardiovascular collapse caused by inability to move air, leading to rising intrathoracic pressure that impedes blood return to the heart). IV beta-blockade in this context can cause fatal **bronchospasm** (sudden, severe constriction of the airway muscles, completely blocking airflow) and cardiovascular collapse. The combination of tachycardia + hypotension + bronchospasm constitutes a physiological state in which beta-blockers are absolutely contraindicated, but no existing system encodes this multi-variable contraindication as a generation constraint.

4. **Communication-agnostic output in a communication-critical setting.**
   - Systems generate output in a single, fixed register regardless of recipient.
   - In the ICU, family conferences about goals of care are among the most communication-sensitive interactions in medicine.
   - Dense medical terminology presented to a family member with limited health literacy increases distress and impedes shared decision-making.
   - *Medical example:* a system generates a prognosis summary for a patient with **anoxic brain injury** (brain damage caused by complete deprivation of oxygen, most commonly after cardiac arrest) after cardiac arrest: "**EEG** [electroencephalogram, a test that records electrical activity in the brain using electrodes placed on the scalp] demonstrates **burst-suppression pattern** [an EEG pattern alternating between brief bursts of electrical activity and periods of near-silence, indicating severe, widespread brain dysfunction] with **generalized periodic discharges** [repetitive, rhythmic electrical patterns occurring across the entire brain, often associated with severe brain injury and poor prognosis]. **Somatosensory evoked potentials** [SSEPs; a test that measures the brain's electrical response to mild electrical stimulation of a peripheral nerve, testing whether sensory signals can travel from the body to the brain cortex] show bilaterally absent **N20 responses** [the specific brain wave that should appear approximately 20 milliseconds after stimulation; bilateral absence means neither side of the brain is receiving sensory input, one of the strongest predictors of poor neurological outcome after cardiac arrest]. **NSE** 85 mcg/L [Neuron-Specific Enolase, a protein released into the blood when neurons are destroyed; levels >33 mcg/L at 72 hours post-arrest are associated with poor neurological outcome] at 72 hours." The attending **neurointensivist** (a neurologist specializing in critical care of brain-injured patients) reads this and understands the implications immediately. The patient's spouse receives the identical output, does not know what "N20 responses" or "NSE" mean, and interprets the clinical language as evasive, leading to the perception that the medical team is withholding information, delaying the **goals-of-care conversation** (a structured discussion between the medical team and the patient's family about treatment priorities, acceptable outcomes, and whether to continue, limit, or withdraw life-sustaining interventions) by 48 hours and prolonging a dying process.

**Common cause:** the field has optimized for task accuracy without jointly optimizing for reasoning verification, structural safety, and communication. These inadequacies cannot be closed independently; they require joint optimization.

## 4. Significance

### 4.1 Clinical Significance
- Hemodynamic instability is among the most time-critical events in acute care medicine; early recognition is directly tied to survival.
  - *Medical example:* a patient developing **cardiogenic shock** (a condition in which the heart suddenly cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, most commonly caused by a massive heart attack, with mortality rates of 40-50% even with treatment) after **acute myocardial infarction** (AMI; a heart attack caused by blockage of a coronary artery, cutting off blood supply to heart muscle) has a window of approximately 60-90 minutes in which initiation of **mechanical circulatory support** (devices such as **Impella** [a miniature pump inserted through the femoral artery into the left ventricle to assist blood flow] or **IABP** [intra-aortic balloon pump, a catheter-mounted balloon placed in the aorta that inflates and deflates in synchrony with the heartbeat to reduce the heart's workload and improve coronary blood flow]) significantly improves survival. A system that detects the hemodynamic trajectory 30 minutes earlier than a conventional early warning score changes the outcome.
- Currently deployed systems either provide no inspectable reasoning (**NEWS2** [National Early Warning Score 2, a standardized scoring system used in hospitals that assigns points based on respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, temperature, systolic blood pressure, heart rate, and level of consciousness to identify patients at risk of deterioration], vendor-embedded deterioration models) or rely on post-hoc audit rather than structural safety.
  - *Medical example:* the **Epic Sepsis Model** (a proprietary machine learning model embedded in the Epic electronic health record system, widely deployed across US hospitals), generates a sepsis risk score without exposing which variables drove the score or whether the underlying physiological pattern is consistent with sepsis rather than an alternative explanation (e.g., **post-surgical inflammatory response** [also called SIRS, Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome; the body's normal inflammatory reaction to surgical trauma, which produces fever, tachycardia, and leukocytosis identical to early sepsis but requires monitoring rather than antibiotics], which shares many of the same vital sign patterns but requires opposite management).
- Direct clinical consequences of the identified failure modes:
  - Context decay: recommendations appropriate 48 hours ago but dangerous now.
    - *Medical example:* recommending continuation of therapeutic **enoxaparin** (a low-molecular-weight heparin, a blood-thinning medication injected subcutaneously to prevent and treat blood clots) in a patient whose **platelet count** has dropped from 180,000 to 45,000/mcL over the past 72 hours (a pattern raising concern for **heparin-induced thrombocytopenia**, HIT; a paradoxical immune reaction to heparin in which the body forms antibodies that activate platelets, causing both a dangerous drop in platelet count and a high risk of life-threatening blood clots; treatment requires immediate discontinuation of all heparin products and initiation of an alternative anticoagulant such as argatroban), because the model's effective context no longer includes the baseline platelet count.
  - Mechanistic hallucination: interventions common in clinical text but physiologically impossible for a specific multi-organ pattern.
    - *Medical example:* recommending **NSAID** analgesia (**ketorolac** 30 mg IV; a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug that provides powerful pain relief but works by inhibiting prostaglandins, which are critical for maintaining blood flow to the kidneys) for post-operative pain in a patient with **acute kidney injury** (AKI; creatinine 4.2 mg/dL, up from 1.1 baseline; normal creatinine is 0.7-1.3 mg/dL; a value of 4.2 indicates severe kidney impairment), because NSAIDs for post-operative pain is among the most common associations in clinical text, but NSAID administration in AKI further reduces renal blood flow and risks **permanent renal damage** requiring lifelong dialysis.
  - Communication-agnostic output: prognostic information in a register an exhausted family member cannot parse.
    - *Medical example:* presenting **APACHE IV** (Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation IV; a severity-of-illness scoring system used in ICUs that predicts hospital mortality based on physiological variables, age, chronic health conditions, and reason for admission) mortality probability as "estimated 72-hour mortality 0.43 [95% CI: 0.31-0.55]" to a family member who does not understand **confidence intervals** (a statistical range indicating that, if the analysis were repeated many times, 95% of the resulting intervals would contain the true value) and hears only the number "43," interpreting it as a test score ("43 out of 100, so failing?") rather than a probability of death.
- MIMIC-IV analysis confirms 71.9% of admissions with the **hemodynamic-instability triad** (the simultaneous occurrence of tachycardia [heart rate >100], hypotension [MAP <65 mmHg], and hyperlactatemia [lactate >2 mmol/L]) have a cardiac-relevant diagnosis (45.6% cardiac-only, 26.3% both cardiac and sepsis).
- High-prevalence condition with substantial cardiovascular involvement, directly relevant to the NHLBI mission.

### 4.2 Methodological Significance
- The limitations above characterize the field as a whole, not any single system.
- No existing framework integrates longitudinal context management, physiologically grounded safety verification, and audience-adapted communication within a jointly optimized architecture.
  - *Medical example:* a patient with **acute-on-chronic heart failure** (a sudden worsening of pre-existing heart failure), baseline **CKD stage 3** (Chronic Kidney Disease stage 3; **GFR** [glomerular filtration rate, the rate at which the kidneys filter blood; normal >90 mL/min] between 30-59 mL/min, indicating moderate kidney impairment), and **new-onset atrial fibrillation** (a common irregular heart rhythm in which the heart's upper chambers quiver chaotically instead of contracting in an organized fashion) requires a system that simultaneously (a) tracks the 10-day trajectory of **creatinine** (kidney function), **BNP** (B-type natriuretic peptide; a hormone released by the heart in response to stretching of heart muscle; elevated levels indicate heart failure severity), and **fluid balance** (the net difference between fluids administered and fluids excreted, critical for managing both heart failure and kidney disease) (context management), (b) verifies that any recommended intervention (**anticoagulation** for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation, **rate control** to slow the heart, **diuresis adjustment** to manage fluid overload) is physiologically compatible with the full multi-organ state (safety verification), and (c) presents the care plan differently to the **cardiology attending**, the overnight **cross-cover resident** (a physician covering patients they do not normally follow, during off-hours), and the patient's **health-care proxy** (a person legally designated to make medical decisions on behalf of a patient who cannot make decisions for themselves) (communication adaptation). No system does all three.
- These components each exist in separate literatures but have never been combined; their interactions remain unstudied.
  - *Medical example:* it is unknown whether constraining a model's output for physiological safety (e.g., blocking a recommendation for aggressive fluid resuscitation in a patient with pulmonary edema) affects the model's ability to communicate that same decision empathetically to a family member ("We are not giving more IV fluids because your father's lungs are already struggling with the fluid he has"). The safety constraint and the communication adaptation may reinforce each other, compete for model capacity, or be entirely independent, but this interaction has never been measured.
- The gap becomes more consequential as model capability improves:
  - A 2026 physician-led evaluation found a frontier model outperforming physician baselines across five reasoning experiments.
  - The accompanying commentary stated explicitly that strong benchmark performance does not establish readiness for clinical use.
  - As models become more capable, the probability that a physiologically dangerous recommendation is also linguistically convincing **increases**, making the absence of structural safety more dangerous, not less.
    - *Medical example:* a more capable model does not simply recommend "give potassium" to a hyperkalemic patient; it generates a clinically detailed, persuasive paragraph: "Given the trend of improving renal function and the patient's history of hypokalemia requiring supplementation, a cautious repletion of 20 mEq KCl IV over 2 hours is warranted, with repeat **BMP** [Basic Metabolic Panel; a blood test measuring sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, BUN, creatinine, and glucose] in 4 hours." Every element of this recommendation sounds reasonable in isolation, and a tired resident at 4:00 AM may follow it without noticing that the "trend of improving renal function" is a hallucination and the most recent potassium is 6.1 mEq/L. Administering IV potassium to a patient with K+ 6.1 risks **fatal cardiac arrhythmia** (potassium above 6.0 mEq/L destabilizes the electrical conduction system of the heart, potentially causing **ventricular fibrillation** [a chaotic, uncoordinated quivering of the heart's lower chambers that produces no blood flow and is fatal within minutes without defibrillation] or **asystole** [complete cessation of all electrical activity in the heart]).
- Evaluation-adequacy problem: not only do current systems fail to address these failure modes, but the tools to detect them do not yet exist at the field level.
